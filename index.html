The Effect of Different Types of Oscillation on the Sedimentation Rate of Fine Sand in Water

Brenden D'Souza

Background

Sediment flow is an interesting part of science, whether it is in the oceans, seas, rivers, or coasts and beaches of the Earth. Understanding how sediments move in different conditions is key to undertakings in adjacent fields. This research aims to test how sand moves, specifically how fast it settles, under different oscillatory conditions: orbital and linear. Oscillatory movement in fluids is commonly associated with high amounts of turbulence, with orbital movement being observed with a higher Reynolds number than linear movement and a much higher Reynolds number than no movement, where Reynolds number is essentially a measure of turbulence. By designing, modelling, 3D printing, and assembling a custom orbital shaker and a custom linear shaker, water with sand was able to oscillate and be observed. By testing three conditions — orbital, linear, and no oscillation — the differences in sedimentation rate of the sand were able to be observed. It showed results similar to the hypothesis although not exactly matching; orbital and linear oscillation had significantly slower sedimentation rates than no oscillation, but there was no significant difference between their sedimentation rates. The data suggests that any sort of forceful movement results in slower sedimentation rates. This research can help to further develop models of different types of flow and can help to understand currents and coastal patterns in a variety of situations.
