The Effect of Different Types of Oscillation on the Sedimentation Rate of Fine Sand in Water

Brenden D'Souza

Background

Sediment transport is an interesting area of science that occurs in oceans, seas, rivers, and coastal environments. Understanding how sediments move under different conditions is important in related fields, such as environmental science and coastal engineering. Oscillatory movement in fluids can create disturbances and may contribute to turbulence. To describe and predict different types of flow, scientists often use the Reynolds number. Different types of oscillation, such as orbital and linear oscillation, can affect the movement of fluid and sediment in different ways. These movements can create different levels of turbulence depending on the conditions. In the absence of fluid motion, the Reynolds number would be zero. By observing how sand moves under orbital and linear oscillation, the effects of each type of motion on turbulence and sediment transport can be compared. This provides a broader understanding of how fluid movement influences sediment transport.

Rationale

While previous studies have examined how individual sediment particles move under various forces, little experimental research directly compares how different oscillation types alter settling behavoir. Understanding whether orbital and linear motions produce distinct settling rates helps clarify how disturbances caused by waves keep particles suspended. By differentiating between these two different modes and comparing them to a stationary control, this study provides empirical insight into fluid-sediment dynamics that can help with coastal engineering, shoreline erosion models, and even marine habitat conservation.
